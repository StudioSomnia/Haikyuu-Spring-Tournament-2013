# HAIKYUU — Spring Tournament 2013

A modern, fully procedural 3D volleyball game for **Godot 4.7**, built as a remake of the
classic *International Volleyball 2009* formula with Haikyuu!! schools, players, and
team-play systems.

Everything you see and hear is generated by code: meshes, materials, crowd, banners,
lighting, UI, sound effects, and animations. There are **no imported models, textures,
music, or other copyrighted assets** in this repository.

---

## Quick start

1. Install **Godot 4.7** (or a later 4.x build) — <https://godotengine.org/download>.
2. Double-click **`run.bat`**.

`run.bat` finds Godot automatically (WinGet install, `PATH`, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Godot`,
`C:\Godot`, and common folders). If it cannot find one, set the path yourself:

```bat
set HYK_GODOT=C:\Godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe
run.bat
```

You can also open `project.godot` in the Godot editor and press **F5**.

### Built-in self-tests

```bat
run.bat --autotest     :: full AI-vs-AI match, prints the result dictionary
run.bat --matchtest    :: human-controlled path with simulated input (serve, receive, spike, pause, subs)
run.bat --uitest       :: walks every menu, starts a tournament, advances a round, opens results
```

Each test runs headless, prints `[...]` log lines, and exits by itself.

| Pause / stats / substitutions | `Esc` or `P` | Start |
| Change camera | `V` | RB |
| Serve type (while serving) | `1`–`3` | — |
| Block scheme (while defending) | `1`–`4` | — |
| Play call (after your receive) | `1`–`5` | — |
| Aim | Mouse | — |

### How a possession plays out

1. **Serve** — hold `Space` to charge, release inside the green band. While you are serving,
   `1`–`3` selects **OVERHAND**, **UNDERARM**, or **JUMP**. `4` and `5` are deliberately
   inactive in this phase. Serve skill controls accuracy and the green window; attack skill
   controls how much power the serve carries.
2. **Reception** — the team picks the nearest receiver and runs them to the landing ring
   automatically. You only time the contact: press `Space` as the ball arrives. A white ring
   closes on the centre as a second timing cue. **Receive** sets the ring size, the timing
   window, the emergency reach, and how quickly you recover from a dive. `Ctrl`/`C` dives at
   the drop point for a longer reach. A clean receive hangs in the air; a shanked one is flat
   and forces the setter to scramble.
3. **Set** — once your receive is controlled, `1`–`5` opens the attack calls. The setter runs
   the called play. **Set** skill controls set accuracy, the width of the spike's timing
   window, and how reliably the AI setter runs quick plays.
4. **Attack** — in `set_flight`, watch the red-yellow-green bar and press `Space` in the
   green. Aim with the mouse, and press `E` to switch between a **full swing** and a **tip**.
   The spike is a physical projectile: it can clear the tape, hit the net, or land out. A
   badly timed press is a weak swing; if you never press in the window, the ball drops for
   the opponent. **Attack** and **jump** set the spike's power and speed; a strong hitter
   wins more dice contests against the block. If the mouse is not over the opponent's court,
   the hit is placed automatically.
5. **Block / dig** — while the opponent has the ball, `1`–`4` calls **READ**, **COMMIT**,
   **SPREAD**, or **TRIPLE**. The front row forms one wall and jumps together; the rest of the
   team shifts its floor coverage around that wall. At the net, attack and block are rolled as
   d6 contests: attack rating + d6 + spike power + jump timing against block rating + d6 + how
   smothered the shot is + block durability + scheme. **Block** skill widens each blocker's
   span and increases durability. A block win is a **live physical deflection** back into the
   attacker's court: wide touches can be saved with a good read or a dive, while a clean stuff
   lands short and hard. The receive ring is still your contact contract: press `Space` as the
   ball drops, or dive to cover the extra ground.

The timing bar is red → yellow → green → yellow → red. The green is the perfect window and
grows with the relevant stat: serve skill for serves, the setter's set skill for spikes, and
the receiver's read for contacts.

The number keys are strictly context-locked: **1–3 while serving**, **1–4 throughout the
opponent's possession**, and **1–5 only after your side has controlled the receive**. A key
from another phase does nothing. The reticle stays on screen all rally — bright while aiming,
dimmed with a dark outline while it is just your cursor.

## Systems

### Match rules
- Rally scoring, sets to **25** (deciding set to **15**), win by 2.
- Best-of-**1 / 3 / 5** sets (selectable).
- Six rotations, side-out rotation, two timeouts and six substitutions per set.
- Jump height is capped at **half a player's body height**, so spikes, blocks, and jump
  serves stay grounded instead of floating.
- **Liberos sit on the bench for now.** The engine does not yet run the front-row
  replacement and serve restrictions a libero needs, so they are excluded from the
  starting six and cannot be substituted in.

### Starting lineups
On the team-select screen press **EDIT LINEUP** for either team. Pick a starter and then a
bench player to swap them, or pick two starters to exchange their rotation slots (P1–P6).
The rotation runs clockwise and the server is always the P1 (bottom-right) player.
Lineups are saved to `user://starting_lineups.json`; **RESET TO DEFAULT** restores the
original six.

### Stats at work
Every action now reads the relevant stat instead of using a fixed success rate:
- **Serve** — accuracy, serve timing window, and power. Underarm is the safe low-power
  option; overhand adds more attack stat; jump has the highest ceiling but punishes poor
  servers with net/long errors.
- **Attack** — spike speed, block-contest strength, and how badly a dig is rattled.
- **Block** — wall spacing, individual reach (span), durability, and the block dice.
- **Set** — set accuracy, spike timing window, and AI play selection.
- **Receive** — contact circle, timing window, emergency reach, and dive recovery.
- **Speed** — raw movement speed and how quickly players close on a ball.
- **Jump** — spike power and timing quality.

At the net, attack and block use a d6 contest. The attacker rolls attack rating + d6 + spike
power + jump timing; the wall rolls block rating + d6 + how smothered the shot is + durability
+ scheme. A clean block win drives the ball down short and hard; a glancing win is a wide,
live deflection that the attacking team can still save.

### Ball model
Serves, passes, and sets stay on deterministic arcs so the rhythm and AI reads are reliable.
**Attacks and block deflections are physical**: they are launched with real velocity, gravity,
and a 120 Hz substepped flight, then collide with the floor and the net. The receive marker
and AI use the current short-horizon landing estimate rather than a fixed scripted endpoint.
This means a spike can genuinely miss the net, clip it, or sail long; a block touch can
deflect wide and stay playable; and a completely missed swing drops rather than auto-hitting.

### Play calls
`QUICK`, `BACK QUICK`, `WING`, `PIPE`, `DUMP` — press `1`–`5` after your receive. Each
changes the set target, tempo, and how the block reads you. Fast attacks beat slow blocks but
demand a clean pass.

### Serve types
Press `1`–`3` while serving: **UNDERARM** (safest, low power, tiny stat barrier, excellent
control), **OVERHAND** (more attack stat, more sensitive to serve skill), **JUMP** (highest
power ceiling, largest serve-skill check — poor servers will net or sail it). `4`–`5` are
inactive during the serve.

### Block schemes
Press `1`–`4` while the opponent has the ball: **READ** (balanced two-man), **COMMIT**
(one blocker sells out on the middle), **SPREAD** (wide wall against pin hitters), **TRIPLE**
(three-man roof, vulnerable to tips). The block is a single unit — the chosen front-row
players travel to the net together, and the remaining players fan into short and deep
coverage lanes around the wall. The enemy AI uses the same wall and chooses its scheme from
your called play, so both sides block with the same logic.

### Reception formations
`BALANCED`, `PERIMETER`, `STACK`.

### FLOW and signature abilities
Every rally adds or removes FLOW for each team. Fill the bar, then press `E` to trigger the
school's signature ability for the next three rallies:

| School | Ability | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Karasuno | FREAK QUICK | Huge bonus on quick attacks |
| Nekoma | FLOOR FORTRESS | Reception and digs become walls |
| Aoba Johsai | SERVICE ACE | Serve pressure spike |
| Shiratorizawa | LEFT CANNON | Left-side attacks overpower the block |
| Fukurodani | MOOD SWING | Attack bonus while the team is hot |
| Date Tech | IRON WALL | Blocks close faster and harder |
| Inarizaki | TWIN DECOY | Attack bonus and a confused block |
| Kamomedai | TOTAL DEFENSE | Receive, dig, and block all improve |
| Johzenji | FREE SPIRIT | Unpredictable attack bonus |
| Wakutani Minami | ACE'S WILL | Ace attacks hit harder |

### Player growth inside a match
Stamina drains with jumping and diving; receivers sprint automatically, so repeated dives
cost them. Tired players move slower and hit weaker. Rotate, call timeouts, and use
**Substitutions** from the pause menu.

**Timeouts** — two per set, called from the pause screen. They restore stamina across the
floor and add FLOW.

**Substitutions** — six per set. Open the pause menu and use the two dropdowns under
**MAKE A SUBSTITUTION**: the left list is the player coming off, the right list is the
bench player coming on, then press **SWAP**. Bench players keep their own stamina, and
tired starters rotate back onto the bench. **AUTO SUB** picks the freshest same-role
player if you do not want to choose. Liberos are shown on the bench but are not selectable.

### Difficulty
Five levels from **VERY EASY** to **VERY HARD** change AI reaction speed, attack accuracy,
serve quality, block reads, and dig reach.

### Cameras
`DYNAMIC`, `BROADCAST`, `BEHIND PLAYER`, `HIGH TACTICAL`.

## Schools

Karasuno, Nekoma, Aoba Johsai, Shiratorizawa, Fukurodani, Date Tech, Inarizaki, Kamomedai,
Johzenji, and Wakutani Minami — each with its own roster, stats, traits, playbook, colours,
motto, and signature ability.

**Spring Tournament** is an 8-team single-elimination bracket (three rounds) seeded around
your chosen school. Exhibition mode lets you pick both sides.

## Project layout

```
scenes/Main.tscn          minimal bootstrap scene (one Node)
scripts/Main.gd           screen flow, input map, self-tests
scripts/Match.gd          match engine: phases, AI, scoring, input, HUD glue
scripts/Ball.gd           deterministic scripted-arc volleyball
scripts/PlayerRig.gd      procedural low-poly player + animation

```

### Validation for contributors

```bat
godot --headless --path . --editor --quit    :: full parse/import check
godot --headless --path . -- --autotest      :: AI match, must print [match] result
godot --headless --path . -- --matchtest     :: human input path, must score points
godot --headless --path . -- --uitest        :: every screen must build without errors
```

Godot's console build exits with code 1 on Windows even on success; judge runs by the
absence of `SCRIPT ERROR` and the presence of the expected log lines.
